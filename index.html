LAYER 01

Business purpose & occupancy

The exemption is fact-based, not label-based — the 14-day rule is the line. Copy that courts owner-occupants is evidence against it for your whole book. Investor language on every asset.

LAYER 02

State licensing

Advertising into a state you're not licensed in is itself a solicitation trigger. Your licensed-state list becomes a contract exhibit and is mirrored in every platform's geo settings.

LAYER 03

UDAP / FTC Act §5

Your exemption from TILA and RESPA is not an exemption from deceptive-practices law. No guaranteed approval, no pre-approved without underwriting, no rate we can't substantiate.

LAYER 04

ECOA & Fair Housing

ECOA covers business credit, not just consumer — and Fair Housing reaches lending secured by dwellings even when investor-owned. It governs how we target, not just what we say.

LAYER 05

TCPA & CAN-SPAM

Every contact we text has a documented consent basis — including the lists you hand us for reactivation. Working opt-outs, quiet hours, DNC scrubbing. This is where the real exposure lives.

LAYER 06

Platform policy

Business-purpose loans sit inside Meta's Financial Products special ad category — no carve-out. Declarations handled correctly, and Google's financial-services requirements met on every landing page.

LAYER 07

Reviews & endorsements

FTC Endorsement Guides and the 2024 Consumer Reviews Rule. No fabricated reviews, no suppressed ones, disclosures where required — in your marketing and in ours.

Questions we get.

How is this different from a lead vendor?

We don't sell leads. We deliver booked appointments with borrowers who've confirmed a business-purpose deal, a property with price and ARV, a close date, and their experience tier — tracked through to funded loans. Lead vendors sell contact records, often to three to five buyers at once.

What exactly counts as a pre-qualified appointment?

It's written into the agreement, not left to interpretation: a booked appointment with a borrower who has self-reported a business-purpose, non-owner-occupied deal, a property with purchase price and rehab budget or ARV and a close date, a deal size above your minimum, deals completed in the last 24 months, and cash available to close. All self-reported. We screen — you underwrite.

What if you send us borrowers who don't qualify?

You set the bar at onboarding: minimum experience tier, minimum liquidity, loan floor, states. We screen every booking against it. If your underwriting declines someone, that still counts as delivered — underwriting is your decision and your business. Want the bar raised? We'll raise it, and volume drops proportionally. We tell you that in advance rather than argue about it later.

How fast do we see appointments?

Usually week two, from your existing database. Paid search goes live around day 14 to 21 and takes about a month to settle. The guarantee window is 90 days for exactly that reason.

Who owns the ad account and the data?

You do, both. The ad account is created in your name with your billing. Your contacts are yours — export on demand, and complete handover of every contact, conversation, and appointment record if we ever part ways. That's in the contract before anyone asks.

Can this create compliance problems for us?

It's built the other direction. Most agencies think lending compliance is one thing — usually licensing. It's seven: the business-purpose exemption and the 14-day occupancy test, the state licensing map, UDAP under FTC Act §5 (which your exemption does not protect you from), ECOA and Fair Housing in targeting, TCPA and CAN-SPAM on every message we send, platform special-ad-category rules, and the FTC's review and endorsement rules. We build to all seven. Campaigns run only where you're licensed. Every asset carries investment-property language. Every contact we text has documented consent — including lists you provide us. No prohibited claims. And any proof-of-funds letter is issued by you, under your name, never by us.
